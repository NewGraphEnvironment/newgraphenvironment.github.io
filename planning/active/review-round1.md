## Findings

- **[claim]** content/project/open_source_software/index.md:116: "fly, finds the fewest historic provincial airphotos that cover a site, **downloads them** and places each on the map". This drops the "thumbnails" qualifier that the #53 copy carried. `fly_fetch()` can only download `"thumbnail"`, `"flight_log"`, `"calibration"` or `"georef"` (fly `R/fly_fetch.R:50`, `match.arg`), and the README says "Download thumbnails, then georeference them". A reader will take "downloads them" to mean the full-resolution scans. The catalogue table on line 203 still says "thumbnails", so the page disagrees with itself. The trace row in findings.md ("downloads and georeferences thumbnails … holds") verified wording the page no longer uses.

- **[claim]** layouts/index.html:347 (How It Works step 2): "Land-cover change can be mapped between any two years **the satellite record covers**". The Open Source page (line 88) keeps the bound, "any two years that free satellite land-cover maps cover", but the landing page drops it. drift's configured land-cover sources are io-lulc, with `available_years = 2017L:2023L`, and esa-worldcover, with `c(2020L, 2021L)` (drift `R/dft_stac_config.R:44,50`), and its Landsat source is commented out. "The satellite record" reads as decades. findings.md says the copy would say "the imagery covers" because "the period is bounded by the source products". The landing wording lost that bound.

- **[claim]** content/project/open_source_software/index.md:74 (flooded): "Run on coarse elevation data and again on lidar, **the difference points to** the roads, rail grades, dykes and fill that may cut a river off from its floodplain." The source states the direction, and the new copy drops it. flooded README, "Resolution and restoration": "The gap between coarse and fine results runs both ways — lidar mostly finds floodplain the coarse DEM misses — but the floodplain the coarse result maps and the fine one excludes is a diagnostic". Only the coarse-maps/lidar-excludes part of the difference points at fill. The #53 copy said so: "Where the coarse result maps floodplain that lidar excludes". The re-trace row ("holds; the coarse-vs-lidar sentence carries it") missed this.

- **[claim]** content/project/open_source_software/index.md:146 and :180 (water-temp-bc). Line 146: "We built … **an open archive of the hydrometric record**". Line 180: "refreshed monthly and **combined with the historic record**", which drops the #53 qualifier "from Environment and Climate Change Canada's real-time service". The two together read as if water-temp-bc holds BC's full hydrometric record. Its README says it covers "~300 BC hydrometric stations", re-pulls "the full ~18-month window Environment Canada (ECCC) serves", and carries an archive from before the monthly job of "water temperature back to 2002, daily discharge to 2016". wet's README describes it as an "archive of ECCC provisional hydrometric data … that bridges HYDAT and real-time". HYDAT, the record of record, is ECCC's and already open. So "built … an archive of the hydrometric record" also leans toward the claim-what-others-lack framing that the copy rule rules out.
