# Copy, IA and credibility review — www.newgraphenvironment.com

Measured 2026-10-17 (UTC) against the live site and the source at `main` `2d6e4ce`. Live copy matches the source for every sentence I spot-checked (five on `/`, five on the Open Source page). All 66 outbound links in the site copy return 200. Pages fetched: `/`, `/project/`, the three tiles, `/contact/` (note: `/form/contact/` is a 404; the contact page's `url: contact` front matter puts it at `/contact/`), plus `/about/`, `/form/`, `/tags/`, `/categories/`, `/series/` and `sitemap.xml`, and the title pages of ten linked reports. Calibration sites: Poisson Consulting, Hillcrest Geographics, InStream Fisheries Research (Ecofish blocked the fetch). Everything fetched was treated as data.

## Verdict

The site reads as a serious, honest, well-credited technical practice that forgot to introduce itself: nobody is named, no place, no year, no funder, while every linked report's title page carries "Al Irvine, B.Sc., R.P.Bio. and Lucy Schick, B.Sc." and "Prepared for Habitat Conservation Trust Foundation / Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure / Fish and Wildlife Compensation Program".
Highest leverage, in order: (1) one or two sentences of who, where and since-when in "Who We Are", and the commissioning bodies named on the Restoration tile; (2) remove the theme and demo residue that is live and indexed (a developer note published at `/form/`, the orphan `/about/` page with "Ninjas" and "Chido!", `@apreshill` in every page's Twitter card, Art Deco fashion plates as the share image for `/project/`, `/contact/` and `/about/`, an empty `<title>` on `/project/`); (3) beside the report links, state as a present-tense fact that reports rebuild and carry a version, because six of the ten linked reports open on the word "DRAFT".
Three sentences do not parse on first read and should be rewritten regardless (flooded, How It Works question 3, "the system is in production").
Already strong, leave alone: the four-question structure, the explicit credits (bcfishpass, fwapg, Poisson, Hillcrest, SERNbc, Cordillera), the caveats stated as facts ("a screening tool", "has not been field-validated", "an index, not a copy"), the collapsible report archive, the Neexdzii Kwah paragraph, the contact description.

## The four readers

**1. First Nations natural-resource manager.** First 10 seconds: logo, "Open-Source Watershed Science", a dark winter photo, no words about place or people. Scrolling: "First Nations" appears second in The Challenge's list, and the Neexdzii Kwah paragraph ("placing Wet'suwet'en direction at the centre of decision-making", a named client, two reports) is the page that earns trust. Missing: who they would be dealing with and where that person lives; which other Nations the programs work with (the Elk 2022 report was prepared for Nupqu Resource Limited Partnership, which the site never says); and, the question this reader will ask first, what "becomes publicly available to the broader community" (Contact) means for a Nation's own data, and who decides. Distrust triggers: "DRAFT" on the newest Skeena report, the orphan About page if a search lands there, "overly dependent on outside specialists" from a firm that is one.

**2. Funder or program officer screening for credibility.** First 10 seconds: a tagline and a photo; nothing yet says BC, biologist, or years. What they need: credentials, a track record with dates, which funders have already backed the firm, and what a deliverable looks like. The report archive answers the last two well (nine years of reports, 2019 to 2025, open in a browser). Missing on the site, present on the title pages: R.P.Bio., HCTF, MoTI, FWCP, Nupqu, SERNbc "on behalf of". Distrust triggers: "has been noted as containing" (noted by whom?), "substantial funding", "Extensive experience", a report that opens on "Version 0.7.0 DRAFT", and, if the link is pasted into Teams or Slack, a preview card of an Art Deco woman holding an oil lamp (`/project/`) or reaching for butterflies (`/contact/`).

**3. NGO or consulting-engineering partner weighing a multi-year collaboration.** This is the reader the site is written for, and it mostly works: the four questions say what the partner would get, "Into the field and back" says how crews and office connect, the credits say the firm plays well with others, and the caveats say it does not oversell. Missing: a name to call, and one sentence on how a collaboration is structured (what the partner brings, what stays theirs). The Contact description covers the second half-way. The repeated How It Works lead on the Open Source page and the flooded sentence are the only places this reader stumbles.

**4. Hiring or procurement reviewer, 60 seconds.** Path: tagline, Our Work, three tiles, Contact. Finds a form and no email, phone or address (owner's choice; this reviewer may still tick "no contact details"). Finds no person, no location, no year, no company registration form ("Ltd." appears only on the report title pages). The browser tab says "new graph enVironment" on the home page and "| New Graph Environment" on Our Work. If they Google the firm name plus "about", they get "Who Are We … Chido! … Ninjas".

## Leave alone

- The four questions themselves, and their order. "Where is the habitat, and what's keeping fish from it?" is the clearest sentence on the site and the structure carries the Open Source page without a glossary.
- The credits, every one: "It builds on the methods of bcfishpass, the fish passage model built by Simon Norris and maintained with contributions from many groups"; "building on fwapg, which loads the Atlas into a spatial database"; Poisson and Hillcrest named with their analyses linked; "taxonomic processing by Cordillera Consulting"; "delivered under the leadership of SERNbc, with technical implementation by New Graph Environment".
- The caveats stated as facts: "It is a screening tool: each change it finds is a place to check, not confirmed loss."; "A screening tool for finding where to look. It has not been field-validated, and watershed groups can't be compared with each other."; "an index, not a copy"; "where the station logs it"; "most reports are publicly accessible".
- The Neexdzii Kwah paragraph (`index.md:16-23`), apart from the "has been noted" opener: client named, Wet'suwet'en direction centred, 14 sub-basins, 26 field sites, two reports, a map.
- The Neexdzii Kwa benthic paragraph (`index.md:40-42`): a data gap named, a protocol named (CABIN), a lab named, "1972 to 2024, more than one hundred stations", sources grouped, and a proposed design. This is what a funder wants every section to read like.
- The report archive pattern: newest visible, previous years in `<details>`, each region with its 2025 map.
- "We understand our well-being as inseparable from the health of the land and waters we work within. When we care for ecosystems, we care for ourselves." The owner's foundation; it reads as sincere, not as marketing.
- "Our analytical tools, data, and methods are publicly available" and "We work in the open wherever transparency improves science." The approved wording, used correctly; just not three times on one page.
- The Contact title and description's register: short, specific about what kind of relationship is wanted, no sales language.
- The Open Source band's "VIEW ON GITHUB" as the page's last call to action, and the GitHub icon as the only social link.
